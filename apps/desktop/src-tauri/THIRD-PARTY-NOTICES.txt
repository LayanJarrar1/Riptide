Riptide ships two third-party programs next to riptide-desktop.exe for video support. They are
separate executables that Riptide starts as child processes; Riptide does not link to them and
they are not modified.

yt-dlp (yt-dlp.exe)
  Home:     https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp
  License:  The Unlicense (public domain). The bundled yt-dlp.exe is a PyInstaller build and
            contains Python and its libraries under their own licenses; see licenses/yt-dlp-LICENSE.txt.
  Version:  see Settings > About > Video support (the exact release is pinned in
            apps/desktop/scripts/fetch-media-tools.mjs together with its SHA-256).
  Updates:  "Update video support" copies yt-dlp into Riptide's data folder and runs `yt-dlp -U`
            there; the copy next to the app is only replaced by app updates.

FFmpeg (ffmpeg.exe)
  Home:     https://ffmpeg.org
  Build:    https://github.com/BtbN/FFmpeg-Builds (win64, "lgpl" variant: no GPL components)
  License:  GNU Lesser General Public License v2.1 or later; see licenses/ffmpeg-LICENSE.txt.
            The build's configuration and bundled library licenses are listed there as well.
  Source:   the corresponding source is available from the FFmpeg-Builds release that the pinned
            archive came from (the exact release and SHA-256 are in fetch-media-tools.mjs).
  Use:      invoked by yt-dlp to merge separate video and audio streams into one MP4/MKV and to
            extract audio. Riptide never calls it for anything else.

BitTorrent (linked into riptide-desktop.exe)
  Home:     https://github.com/ikatson/rqbit (librqbit 9.0.1 and its librqbit-* sub-crates)
  License:  Apache-2.0. Apache-2.0 code may be included in a GPLv3 work, which is how the
            desktop app is distributed (see docs/decisions.md, D-torrent-1).
  Crypto:   the TLS stack under it uses aws-lc-rs / aws-lc-sys (BoringSSL-derived C code under
            ISC and OpenSSL-style licenses) and ring; their notices ship with the crates and are
            listed by `cargo metadata`.
  Other:    about 340 further Rust crates, all under MIT, Apache-2.0, ISC, BSD, Zlib, Unicode-3.0
            or CDLA-Permissive-2.0 (or a dual license offering one of those); option-ext is
            MPL-2.0. Riptide replaces librqbit's file storage with its own, so file names from a
            torrent are sanitised by Riptide's code, not by the library.

Riptide itself is dual-licensed under MIT or Apache-2.0 (see LICENSE-MIT and LICENSE-APACHE).
